DUE DILIGENCE GUIDE

Due diligence under CRS

Due diligence is how a Reporting Financial Institution identifies which of its accounts are Reportable Accounts. The procedures that apply depend on two things: whether the account is new or pre-existing, and whether it is held by an individual or an entity. That gives four regimes — pick a combination below to see the threshold, the required review, and what makes the account reportable.

Indicia of residence (individual accounts)

When reviewing pre-existing individual accounts by electronic record search, these indicators suggest tax residence in a Reportable Jurisdiction. If any are found, the account is treated as reportable to that jurisdiction — unless it is cured.

Identification of the account holder as a resident of a Reportable Jurisdiction
Current mailing or residence address (including a PO box) in a Reportable Jurisdiction
One or more telephone numbers in a Reportable Jurisdiction and no telephone number in the RFI's own jurisdiction
Standing instructions (other than to a Depository Account) to transfer funds to an account in a Reportable Jurisdiction
A currently effective power of attorney or signatory authority granted to a person with an address in a Reportable Jurisdiction
A hold-mail instruction or in-care-of address in a Reportable Jurisdiction, where the RFI has no other address on file

Curing indicia. The RFI need not treat an account as reportable if it obtains — or has reviewed and retained — a self-certification and/or documentary evidence establishing the account holder's status as not resident in the Reportable Jurisdiction indicated.

Points to watch
Review timelines. Pre-existing High Value individual accounts are generally reviewed within one year of the jurisdiction's implementation date; Lower Value accounts within two years.
Change in circumstances. Any change that adds or contradicts information on the holder's status requires the RFI to obtain a new self-certification or documentary evidence.
Aggregation. Account balances are aggregated across accounts held by the same person, to the extent the RFI's systems link them — this can push an account over a threshold.
Self-certification validity. A self-certification remains valid until a change in circumstances causes the RFI to know, or have reason to know, that it is incorrect or unreliable.

This information is for guidance purposes only and does not constitute legal or tax advice. Thresholds, timelines and optional elections vary by jurisdiction — always confirm against the applicable implementing legislation and the OECD CRS.
