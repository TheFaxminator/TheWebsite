FFI CLASSIFICATION

Where FATCA starts

FATCA divides every non-US entity into two worlds: a Foreign Financial Institution (FFI) or a Non-Financial Foreign Entity (NFFE). If the entity is an FFI, its FATCA status — Reporting FI under an IGA, Participating, Deemed-Compliant, Exempt Beneficial Owner or Nonparticipating — decides its obligations and whether the 30% withholding tax bites. If it is not an FFI, it is an NFFE, which then matters when it holds an account elsewhere. Work through the questions to classify an entity.

FFI FATCA statuses

Once an entity is an FFI, its status determines its registration, due-diligence and reporting duties. The exact categories and conditions are set by the FATCA regulations and, where relevant, the applicable Intergovernmental Agreement.

Reporting FI under an IGA — reports to its local tax authority (Model 1) or directly to the IRS (Model 2)
Participating FFI — has an FFI Agreement with the IRS and reports US accounts directly to it
Registered / Certified Deemed-Compliant FFI — e.g. local FFI, restricted fund, sponsored entity, non-reporting IGA FI, owner-documented FFI
Exempt Beneficial Owner — governmental entities, central banks, international organisations and certain retirement funds
Nonparticipating FFI — not compliant; subject to 30% withholding on withholdable payments

This walkthrough is a simplified guide and does not constitute legal or tax advice. FATCA classification turns on the detailed FFI and NFFE definitions, the US Treasury regulations and any applicable IGA — always confirm the outcome against the applicable rules or a qualified adviser.
